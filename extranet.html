What is an Extranet?

An extranet is a private network that allows authorized external users — such as suppliers, customers, or business partners — to access part of an organization's intranet. It is not open to the general public, but it is not purely internal either. It sits in between.

The key idea is controlled access. A company can decide exactly which parts of their internal network an outsider is allowed to see, and block everything else. Access is always restricted through security methods like usernames, passwords, or encrypted connections.

Easy way to remember: Intranet = internal users only. Extranet = internal network + selected trusted outsiders given limited access.

An extranet allows a company to securely share specific information with external partners without opening up their entire internal network.

What is an Extranet Used For?

Organizations use extranets when they need to share information with people outside the company but still want to keep that sharing controlled and secure. Common uses include:

  • Allowing suppliers to check stock levels or submit invoices.
  • Letting customers track their orders or submit support requests.
  • Sharing documents securely with business partners.
  • Giving distributors access to price lists and ordering systems.
  • Collaborating on projects with external contractors.

How Access is Controlled

Because the extranet connects to outside users, security is very important. Organizations use several methods to control who gets in:

  • Usernames and passwords — only people with valid login details can enter.
  • Firewalls — block unauthorized access attempts from the outside.
  • VPN (Virtual Private Network) — creates an encrypted tunnel so data cannot be intercepted.
  • Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) — adds a second step to the login process for extra security.
  • Role-based access — each external user only sees the specific parts they are permitted to access.

Technologies Used

Like the intranet, an extranet uses standard internet technologies such as TCP/IP and web browsers. The difference is that it extends access beyond the organization's physical network using secure internet connections.

Real-World Example

A large retailer might give its suppliers access to an extranet where they can log in, see current stock levels, and submit delivery confirmations. The suppliers only see what is relevant to them — they cannot access the retailer's internal employee systems or financial records.

In short, an extranet is a practical solution for organizations that need to collaborate with outsiders without compromising the security of their internal network.
